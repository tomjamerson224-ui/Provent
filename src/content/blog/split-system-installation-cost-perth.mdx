## What's a back-to-back install?

The indoor unit goes on an **outside wall**, and the outdoor unit sits **directly behind it** on the other side of that wall. The pipe run is short and straight, which makes it the quickest and cheapest way to install a split system.

If the room doesn't have a suitable outside wall, the pipes need to run further — that's where the extras below come in.

## What affects the price?

1. **Size (kW).** Bigger rooms need bigger units, and bigger units cost more.
2. **Brand and model.** Premium models cost more but are typically quieter, more efficient and come with Wi-Fi control.
3. **Where the units go.** Back-to-back is cheapest. Internal walls, long pipe runs, or an outdoor unit on the other side of the house add cost.
4. **Mounting.** Ground level is standard; wall brackets and above-head-height mounting add a little.
5. **Electrical.** Larger units may need their own circuit from the switchboard. We'll check your switchboard when we quote.

## What size do you need?

As a rough guide:

- **2–2.5kW** — nursery, study or small bedroom (up to about 20m²)
- **3.5kW** — a standard bedroom or small living area (20–30m²)
- **5kW** — master bedroom or open-plan living (30–45m²)
- **7kW and up** — large open-plan kitchen and living areas (45m²+)

Sun, ceiling height and insulation all change the answer, so try our [split system size calculator](/split-system-size-calculator) for a better estimate.

## Is one split enough, or do you need more?

If you're looking at three or more split systems, compare the total with a [multi-head system](/blog/multi-head-split-system) or [ducted air conditioning](/ducted-air-conditioning).
