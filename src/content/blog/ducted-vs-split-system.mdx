It's the first question almost every Perth homeowner asks us: **should I get ducted air conditioning or split systems?** There's no single right answer — it depends on how many rooms you want comfortable, how you use the house, your budget and how you feel about units on the walls.

Here's how the two compare, with the real installed prices we charge in Perth.

## The short answer

- **Choose ducted** if you want most of the house heated and cooled evenly, you'd rather not see units on the walls, and you're happy to invest more upfront.
- **Choose split systems** if you only need one to three rooms done, you want the lowest upfront cost, or you're adding cooling to an extension, granny flat or single room.

## How each system works

A **split system** has an indoor unit mounted high on the wall and an outdoor unit outside, connected by refrigerant pipes. Each split heats or cools the room it's in.

A **ducted system** has one large indoor unit hidden in the roof space and an outdoor unit outside. Insulated ducts carry conditioned air to slim vents in the ceiling of each room, and a return air grille draws air back to be re-conditioned. With **zoning**, you choose which areas get air — for example, living areas by day and bedrooms at night.

## Ducted vs split: side by side

| | Ducted | Split system |
|---|---|---|
| Rooms covered | Whole home (zoned) | One room per indoor unit |
| What you see inside | Slim ceiling vents | A unit on each wall |
| Upfront cost | Higher | Lowest for 1–3 rooms |
| Running cost | Low when zoned well | Lowest for single rooms |
| Noise indoors | Very quiet | Quiet, but the unit is in the room |
| Install time | Usually half a day | A few hours per unit |
| Buyer appeal | Often expected in larger family homes | Fine for smaller homes |

## Running costs

The biggest myth is that ducted is always expensive to run. It isn't — **if it's zoned and sized correctly**:

- A split system running in one room uses less power than a ducted system running the whole house. If you genuinely only use one or two rooms, splits win.
- Once you're running three or more splits at the same time, a properly zoned ducted system running the same areas is usually comparable or better, and it keeps the house far more even.
- Zoning is the key. Running only the living zone during the day and the bedroom zone at night is how ducted stays economical. A smart controller like the [AirTouch 5](/blog/airtouch-5-review) makes that easy.

## Comfort and looks

Ducted wins on comfort and aesthetics. Air comes from the ceiling, temperatures stay even through the house, and the only things you see are slim vents — or sleek [linear vents](/projects/modern-linear-vents) if you want a cleaner look.

Split systems are effective but you'll feel the airflow from one point in the room, and many people simply don't like the look of a unit on every bedroom wall.

## When split systems make more sense

- You only need **one to three rooms** done
- You're cooling an **extension, granny flat, studio or garage**
- The roof space is **too tight for ductwork** (we'll tell you honestly after an inspection)
- You want the **lowest upfront cost**
- You're renting or planning to sell soon

If you need several rooms on one outdoor unit, a [multi-head split system](/blog/multi-head-split-system) sits in between the two.

## When ducted makes more sense

- You want **most of the house** comfortable, including bedrooms at night
- You're **replacing an old evaporative cooler** — see our [evaporative to ducted conversions](/evaporative-to-ducted-reverse-cycle)
- You're **renovating or building** and want a clean, finished look
- It's a **4+ bedroom** family home where the cost per room evens out

## Our take

For most family homes in Perth that want whole-of-house comfort, **ducted reverse cycle with good zoning** is the better long-term system. For smaller homes, units or targeted cooling, **split systems** are hard to beat on value.
