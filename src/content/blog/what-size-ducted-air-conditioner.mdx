Getting the size right is the single most important decision in a ducted install. Too small and it struggles on a 40°C Perth afternoon. Too big and you've paid extra for a system that cycles on and off and never quite feels comfortable.

Here's a practical guide to ducted sizes by bedroom count — the same starting points we use before we measure up a home.

## Ducted sizes and prices by home size

These are starting points for **single-storey** homes. The right size for *your* home comes from the floor area and the factors below, which is why we always measure up before giving a fixed quote.

## What size for a 3 bedroom house?

Most **2–3 bedroom** homes need **7–10kW**. A compact 3x1 with standard ceilings and good insulation sits at the lower end. A 3x2 with a big open-plan kitchen and living area, or lots of glass facing west, sits at the upper end.

## What size for a 4 bedroom house?

A typical **4 bedroom** single-storey home needs **10–14kW**. This is the most common ducted install we do in Perth. The usual variables are the size of the open-plan living area, whether there's a theatre or activity room, and ceiling height.

## What size for a 5 bedroom house?

**Five bedrooms or more** usually means **16kW+**. At this size it's worth thinking carefully about zoning, and on larger or double-storey homes, whether one system or two makes more sense.

## What changes the size you need?

Bedroom count is only a rough guide. These are the things that actually move the number:

1. **Floor area.** A 4 bedroom home can be 160m² or 280m². Floor area matters more than bedroom count.
2. **Ceiling height.** Raked, high or 2.7m+ ceilings mean more air to condition.
3. **Open-plan living.** Big combined kitchen/living/dining spaces need more capacity than closed rooms.
4. **Sun and glass.** West-facing windows, big sliding doors and limited eaves add a lot of heat in a Perth summer.
5. **Insulation.** A well-insulated roof can let you drop a size. No ceiling insulation can push you up one.
6. **Zoning.** You don't need to cool every room at once, so a well-zoned system can often be smaller than the "whole house" figure suggests. See [how many zones you need](/blog/how-many-zones-ducted-air-conditioning).
7. **Double storey.** Heat rises — upstairs bedrooms need more capacity, and the layout decides whether one system can do both levels.

## What about double-storey homes?

Double-storey homes vary a lot. If there's a way to run a duct downstairs, one system can often do both levels. If not — and there's a real ceiling space on each level — upstairs and downstairs may need separate units. We need to inspect the home before we can size or price it.

## Why "rule of thumb" sizing isn't enough

You'll see online calculators that multiply your floor area by a number. They're fine for a ballpark, but they can't see your west-facing glass, your roof insulation or the way you actually use the house. Every ducted system we install is sized from a measure-up of the actual home — and you get a written quote before any work starts.

## The bottom line

- **2–3 bedroom:** 7–10kW, around $7,000–$12,000 installed
- **4 bedroom:** 10–14kW, around $10,000–$15,000 installed
- **5+ bedroom:** 16kW+, from $16,000 installed
- **Double storey:** inspection first
