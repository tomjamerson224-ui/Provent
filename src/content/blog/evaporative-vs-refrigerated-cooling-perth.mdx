Drive down almost any older Perth street and you'll see evaporative coolers on the roofs. They suit our hot, dry summers — but more and more homeowners are replacing them with **reverse cycle refrigerated air conditioning**. Here's how the two really compare.

## How each one works

**Evaporative cooling** draws hot outside air through wet pads on the roof. As the water evaporates it cools the air, and a fan pushes that cooler air through ducts into the house. You leave doors or windows partly open so warm air can escape.

**Refrigerated (reverse cycle) air conditioning** uses a compressor and refrigerant to remove heat from the air inside your home, the same way a fridge works. Reverse cycle can also run backwards to **heat** the house in winter. Doors and windows stay closed.

## Side by side

| | Evaporative | Reverse cycle refrigerated |
|---|---|---|
| Cools on dry days | Very well | Very well |
| Cools on humid days | Struggles | Yes |
| Heats in winter | No | Yes |
| Windows | Need to be open | Closed |
| Fresh air | Constant outside air | Recirculated, filtered |
| Running cost (cooling) | Lower | Higher, but zoning helps |
| Upfront cost | Lower | Higher |
| Dust, smoke & noise from outside | Comes in with the air | Kept out |
| Water use | Uses water | None |

## Where evaporative still makes sense

- **Lowest running cost** for cooling on dry days
- You **like fresh air** moving through the house
- You already have **separate heating** you're happy with
- Workshops, sheds and spaces that are open anyway

If you're happy with evaporative and just need a new unit, we supply and install those too — see [evaporative air conditioning](/evaporative-air-conditioning).

## Why Perth homes are switching to reverse cycle

1. **Heating as well as cooling.** One system does both, so many people retire an old gas or electric heater at the same time.
2. **Humid days.** When Perth gets muggy, evaporative cooling barely takes the edge off. Refrigerated air keeps working.
3. **Windows closed.** Better for security, dust, bushfire smoke, pollen and street noise.
4. **Zoning.** Cool just the living areas by day and bedrooms at night, controlled from your phone.
5. **Comfort.** Refrigerated air dehumidifies as it cools, so the house feels drier and more comfortable.

## Can you reuse the evaporative ducts?

No. Evaporative systems move a large volume of air through **big ducts that are too large for a reverse cycle system**, so new, correctly sized insulated ducting goes in.

What you *can* often keep is the **outlet positions**. Best practice is to move the outlets out towards the outside walls of the house, where they work best for reverse cycle — but if you'd rather not patch the ceiling, we can put the new outlets in the same spots.

## Which should you choose?

- **Stay evaporative** if you mainly want cheap cooling on dry days, like fresh air, and have heating sorted.
- **Switch to reverse cycle** if you want heating too, hate humid days, want the windows shut, or want room-by-room control.

For most Perth families we talk to, the deciding factor is winter heating — once one system does both, the switch usually makes sense.
