A **multi-head split system** runs several indoor units — "heads" — from a single outdoor unit. It sits somewhere between separate split systems and full ducted air conditioning, and in the right home it's a great solution.

Here's how it works, what it costs in Perth, and when it's the right choice.

## How a multi-head split system works

A normal split system is one indoor unit connected to one outdoor unit. A multi-head (or "multi-split") system connects **two or more indoor units to one larger outdoor unit**. Each indoor head has its own controls, so every room can be set to its own temperature, and rooms you're not using can be switched off.

## Multi-head vs separate split systems

| | Multi-head | Separate split systems |
|---|---|---|
| Outdoor units | One | One per room |
| Cost per room | Higher | Usually lower |
| If the outdoor unit fails | All connected rooms affected | Only one room affected |
| Outside wall space | Minimal | Needs room for each unit |
| Pipe runs | Longer, all back to one unit | Short, back-to-back where possible |
| Flexibility to add later | Limited by outdoor unit capacity | Add another split any time |

## When a multi-head makes sense

- **Only room for one outdoor unit** — courtyards, townhouses, apartments with a small balcony, or strata rules limiting outdoor units
- **You want fewer units on the outside** of the house for looks or noise
- **Two to four rooms** need heating and cooling, but ducted isn't practical (no roof space, a flat roof, or a concrete ceiling)

## When separate splits are better

- **Budget** — two or three separate back-to-back splits usually cost less
- **Reliability** — if one breaks down, the rest of the house is still comfortable
- **Rooms are spread out** with easy outside walls for each outdoor unit
- **You might add rooms later** — a new split can go in any time

## When ducted is better

If you're looking at **four or five heads**, it's worth comparing with [ducted air conditioning](/ducted-air-conditioning). At that size, a ducted system often costs about the same, conditions the house more evenly, and you don't have a unit on every wall. ## The bottom line

Multi-head split systems are a smart fix when there's only space for one outdoor unit or you want a cleaner outside wall. For most homes with easy outside walls, separate split systems are cheaper and more resilient — and once you're at four or more rooms, ducted is worth a serious look.
